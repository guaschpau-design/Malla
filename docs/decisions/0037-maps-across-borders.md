# 0037. Maps across Spain, Portugal and southern France

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-27

## Context

The street map already reaches 44° N, so teams near Portugal or in the French Pyrenees see it on both sides of the border. Everything else stopped at Spain's border: the orthophotos and the topographic map came from Spain's IGN alone (ADR-0023, ADR-0034), the relief shading from IGN's terrain model, and the measurements (line of sight, climbs) from the coarse Terrain Tiles (ADR-0024).

Open services cover the rest:

- **Portugal.** DGT publishes OrtoSat 2023, 30 cm satellite orthoimagery of the mainland, as a WMS in Web Mercator under CC BY 4.0, transparent outside Portugal.
- **France.** IGN France publishes BD ORTHO through its Géoplateforme WMTS under the Licence Ouverte 2.0. It answers 404 far from France, but paints white the parts of its border tiles that lie in Spain.
- **Spain.** PNOA reaches a kilometre or two beyond the border, and further out is blurred Sentinel-2 or a flat colour.
- **Elevation.** Mapterhorn publishes elevation tiles of the world, 512 pixels wide in the Terrarium encoding. They are built from each country's best open model: Spain's, Portugal's and France's LiDAR models, and Copernicus GLO-30 elsewhere. They are free to use with attribution.

## Decision

- **One orthophoto source.** It takes each tile from the country it lies in. A tile across a border is joined on the device:
  - Spain's tile first;
  - Portugal's over it, since its service leaves the rest transparent;
  - France's clipped to France's outline, since its service paints the rest white.

  The outlines of mainland Portugal and of France up to 44° N come from Natural Earth (public domain), drawn 300 m inside the border and generated by a script (`apps/web/scripts/make-borders.py`). Further out than zoom 8, where the three are alike, Spain's service alone shows the whole area; from zoom 8 each country's photographs are sharper beyond Spain than Spain's coarse Sentinel-2 mosaic there.

- **One elevation source.** Mapterhorn's tiles, up to zoom 13 (about 10 m a pixel), are the elevation for everything:
  - the relief shading;
  - the contour lines;
  - the line of sight, now sampled every 10 m;
  - the climbs.

  This replaces both IGN's terrain model and the Terrain Tiles.

- **Contour lines from that elevation.** The device works out the topographic map's contour lines with maplibre-contour, through our tile protocol, so they work offline where the elevation was downloaded:
  - every 100 m on a regional view;
  - every 25 m at a town's zoom;
  - every 10 m closer;
  - every index line carries its height.

  IGN's vector map is kept for the names of the terrain in Spain.

- **France's cadastre.** The cadastre layer also shows France's, from its Géoplateforme WMS, in the same way as Spain's (ADR-0036). Portugal has no national cadastre service to show.
- **Map database version 4.** It renames the sources (`ign-contours` → `ign-names`, `ign-ortho` → `ortho`) and drops the elevation tiles of the former encodings, which have to be downloaded again.

## Consequences

- The satellite map, the topographic map, the relief and the line of sight work alike on both sides of the border, and the line of sight is measured on data three times finer.
- Contour lines are the same everywhere; in Spain they are no longer IGN's official ones, but are drawn from a model as detailed.
- Working out contour lines takes the device some time per tile, at most a few tiles at once.
- Border tiles are joined on the device, so they cost three downloads instead of one.
- Mapterhorn is a young project run by volunteers. If it went, the same files are published as PMTiles and could be served from our own server.
