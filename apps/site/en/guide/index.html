Preparation, operation and deployment.

How to prepare the team, what to do during an operation and how to stay coordinated when there is no coverage. No jargon.

What Tesel is

Tesel is a situational awareness tool for teams. It brings together on one shared map every member's position, what has been marked on the ground (sectors, meeting points, hazards), the team's messages, tasks and reports. If someone needs help, they press SOS and the whole team knows at once, with their position.

With internet it works like any messaging app. Without internet it keeps working with pocket radios that pass messages to each other.

Tesel does not replace 112.

It helps your team coordinate. If you can call emergency services, always do.

Before: get the team ready

An emergency is no time to learn a new tool. Set aside half an hour on a quiet day.

Everyone

  1. Open tesel.eu/app in your phone's browser and type your name. No email, phone number or password needed.
  2. Add it to your home screen. On Android, with Chrome: menu ⋮ → “Add to Home screen”. On iPhone, with Safari: Share button → “Add to Home Screen”. It then opens like an app and works offline.
  3. Download the map of your area: “More” → “Offline maps”, move the map to your area and tap “Download visible area”. In Spain, Portugal, Andorra and southern France you can also download the topographic and satellite maps. Do it on Wi-Fi, as it takes space. If a download is cut short, “Resume” carries on where it stopped. Even if you download nothing, Tesel keeps an overview of the map and what you last looked at, so the map is never blank without a connection.

The coordinator

  1. Create the group with “Create a group”, on the “To start” card the map shows while you are in no group. Whoever creates it is its coordinator: they invite the others and organise the teams.
  2. Tap “Invite” and share the link, or let people scan the QR code. Whoever has the invitation joins the group, so share it only with those who belong.
  3. Mark the operation area: “Add” → “Area”, draw it and choose “Operation area”. Each member of the group will be offered “Download” to have its map without a connection.
  4. If you are going to use radios, set them up in advance (see Radios).

The whole team

  • Agree on a simple plan: a meeting point, who coordinates and what to do without coverage.
  • Run a drill: send a message, send a test SOS and cancel it, and if you have radios, repeat it with phones in airplane mode and Bluetooth on.
  • Repeat the drill now and then, with charged batteries.
  • To learn the app on your own, tap “Practise on your own” on the map's “To start” card (if you are already in a group: “Groups” → “Your other groups” → “Practise with a simulated group”) and choose a situation (missing person, flood, wildfire, popular race or mountain route, which is played where the map is: take it to some mountains first) and your part: a member, who gets tasks from the coordinator, or the coordinator, who organises the team. It is an exercise of about 25 minutes in your own neighbourhood: the 112 control room and seven simulated teammates, in two teams, set off from the meeting point and walk the streets of the map, which they name as you know them. The situation changes by itself: the water rises from the nearest river or stream and cuts streets off, the fire spreads and turns with the wind, witnesses and neighbours call, the coordinator replans the search. The teammates do the tasks given to them, run out of battery, tire, call an SOS and ask you what to do. Somewhere the mobile network fails: whoever carries a LoRa radio still gets through by radio, and whoever does not is cut off. What you do, or do not do, changes how it ends, and at the end a report tells how it went: a few figures, what you did well and what to do better. The practice's card shows the time, and a five-step guide suggests what to try. It all happens on your phone: nothing leaves it. The practice ends with the exercise, with “End practice”, when you use another group or when you close the app.

During an operation

At the top, next to the logo, is the group you are in: tap it to invite, organise or change group. At the bottom are the SOS button and five buttons: Me, Add (everything new starts here: a marker, a route, a zone, a task or a report), Chat (with a number when there are new messages), Tasks and More. For everything else, touch the map: press and hold a point, or tap a marker or a member, and you will see what you can do there. Whenever you save or send something, a short notice says whether it has reached the group, gone by radio or waits on the phone for a connection.

  • Tell the team how you are in “Me”: at the top, your name (the pencil changes it, and the group sees it at once); your status in one tap (active, resting, off duty) and, all in view, whether you have a LoRa radio, your profile (civil protection, firefighters, police…), your position, battery, what you carry (first-aid kit, water, radio set…) and your tasks. Under “Position”, a switch shares where you are; off, the team sees where you were, unless you turn on “Hide where I was too”. It also tells your coordinates and how precise your GPS is. Tap a teammate on the map to see the same of them; someone resting or off duty shows as an empty ring. An iPhone cannot tell its battery.
  • Teams. Whoever coordinates opens “Groups” → “Organise” → “New team”: they name it, choose a colour and tick who is in it. Members are listed team by team, and on the map each person wears their team's colour. A task or a strip of a sweep can be for a whole team: it counts as each member's task and tells all of them.
  • Write to the group with the “Chat” button. Keep messages short and clear: over radio they cost less and arrive sooner. If you cannot type, tap the microphone in the text box and speak: the message writes itself and you send it once checked. Dictation uses the phone’s speech recognition (Google or Apple) and on most phones needs an internet connection.
  • Situation reports. “Add” → “Report”: choose what is happening (fire, collapse, missing person…), and, if you like, count the casualties by triage colour and mark the dangers, how the access is and what is needed. It goes with your position in one message, by radio too. In the chat each takes one line, which opens when tapped; they stay pinned in the “Reports” tab, with the casualties added up, and show on the map.
  • Mark what matters with “Add”: a marker (a meeting point, a hazard), a route or a zone (a sector). The whole group sees it at once. If you are given coordinates, press the # button on the card and type them instead of tapping the map; the same when picking a task's place. A zone can come from a KML or GeoJSON file too, as routes can.
  • Routes. “Add” → “Route”: tap the map point by point along the way (up to 25 points), then “Finish”. If it comes from a GPS or another app, press the file button (beside undo) and pick a GPX, KML or GeoJSON file: the app keeps 25 points and tells you how far, at most, the route strays from the original. Tap the route and press “Follow route”: a card takes you to its start and then from point to point, with what is left, and warns you if you stray. “Task on this route” hands it to someone; at the end, “Task done” closes it in one tap.
  • Sweeps. Whoever coordinates draws an area styled “Search area, assigned”, taps it and presses “Plan a sweep”. They choose the terrain (open ground, 25 m between searchers; scrub, 10 m; dense forest, 5 m) or any spacing, how many teams and who leads each; “Turn lanes” changes their direction. The lanes show on the map, one coloured strip per team, before “Create sweep”. Each team gets its strip as a task: “Follow strip” guides it lane by lane and tells how much it has swept from its own trail. The area shows how each strip is going, and once all are done it turns “cleared” by itself.
  • Share out the work with “Tasks”: “Check the warehouse”, “Bring water to the meeting point”. Only the text is needed, typed or picked from the common ones (search the area, check a home, treat an injured person…); if you like, say who it is for and give it a place with “Pick on the map” in the task itself. You can also start it from the map: press and hold a point → “Task here”, or tap a marker, a zone or a route, and the task then goes wherever that object goes. Anyone can add, start and finish tasks, and a number on the button tells you how many are yours. Use them the way that suits your team: for everything, for what matters, or not at all.
  • If you need help, press SOS, choose what is happening and send it. It carries your position; if the phone does not know where you are yet, it goes anyway and your position follows as soon as there is one. When someone sees it they tap “Acknowledge” and you will know. Until it has reached someone, the notice says so; by radio it repeats until someone acknowledges it. If it is no longer needed, cancel it.
  • If you see someone else's SOS, tap “Acknowledge” so they know the group has seen it. An SOS makes the phone vibrate and sound, and closes the chat if it was open so that you see it.
  • “Are you OK?” check. Whoever coordinates turns it on in “Groups” → “Organise”. Anyone active who has not moved or touched Tesel for 30 minutes feels the phone vibrate and is asked; a tap on the screen answers. With no answer in 2 minutes, an SOS goes out with their last position. It never asks someone resting or off duty. For now it works only with Tesel open on screen.
  • Danger areas. When someone draws an area in the “Danger area” style, the phone of each person who walks into it vibrates and says which one. Each phone works it out from its own GPS, offline too; while the group has a danger area, the GPS stays on for this, sharing nothing.

Finding your way

The tools in this section work on the phone itself, with or without a group, and with no connection.

  • Where you are. At the top right are the compass, which puts north back up, and the target button, which shows you on the map and keeps the map centred on you. Move the map with your finger and it stops following; press it again to centre. The GPS is only used while something needs it: sharing your position, this button or guidance.
  • Coordinates. Press and hold a point of the map (right click on a computer): you see how far it is, what you can do there and, below, its coordinates; tap them to copy them. The format (degrees, DMS, UTM or MGRS) is chosen in “More” → “Settings” and remembered. If someone reads you coordinates, type them in “More” → “Go to a coordinate” as you were given them and the app finds them on the map.
  • Getting somewhere. A point of the map, a marker or a team member shows how far it is from you and in which direction. With “Guide me”, a card at the top shows the distance and an arrow pointing the way, and a dotted line joins you to the destination. When the destination is a member, it moves with them. While guiding you, the screen stays on.
  • Measuring. “More” → “Measure distances”: tap the map point by point to see the total distance and, from three points, the area they enclose. Zones drawn on the map also show their area.
  • Map type, trails and layers. With the layers button under the target you choose the map by tapping its picture: streets (streets, buildings and roads), topographic (contour lines, paths and the names of places, ravines and springs) or satellite (aerial photographs). The last two cover Spain, Portugal, Andorra and southern France. From afar the satellite map is one Sentinel‑2 image, alike everywhere; close up, each country’s official aerial photographs. Each member leaves a line behind with where they have been in the last hours. Below, each layer is a button that is lit while it is shown: tap it to hide or show members, markers, tasks, reports, trails, services, weather warnings, relief, cadastre, radar or grid. Your own position and SOS calls are always shown. With “Cadastre” on (online, zoomed in) you see the parcels with their numbers and the polygon, as a plot in the countryside is named. With “Relief” on, the map shades hills and valleys and, next to the distance to a place, you see how much the straight way climbs and descends, and its steepest stretch.
  • Radar. In the map layers, “Radar” draws distance rings around you, closer or wider apart with the zoom, and a line to the five nearest teammates with their distance and bearing. While it is on, the GPS stays on.
  • MGRS grid. In the map layers you can draw the MGRS grid, the one fire services, the military and ATAK use: lines every 100 km, 10 km, 1 km or 100 m as the zoom allows, with their digits, and the name of each 100 km square.
  • Services. The map marks health centres and hospitals, pharmacies, fire stations, police, helipads, town halls and fuel stations with round icons: the large ones from afar, the rest when zoomed in to street level. Tap one for its name and to be guided there. They come from OpenStreetMap with the map, so they are also there offline in areas downloaded with “Streets” detail. In some villages the map may be incomplete.
  • Weather warnings. Official yellow, orange and red warnings (AEMET in Spain, IPMA in Portugal, through MeteoAlarm) tint their region on the map. If your location is on and you are inside one, a card at the top says what it warns of and until when; a long press on any point shows the warnings there. The app keeps them, so without coverage you still see the last ones it received. Hide them with “Weather warnings” in the map layers.
  • Who stays in touch without coverage. In “Me” and on each member’s card, the “LoRa radio” line says whether one is linked to their app: those with one stay in touch with the group if the mobile network falls; those without are cut off. The app knows by itself, nothing to tick; if you have none, “Connect” takes you to link one. The card also says when something of theirs last reached you by radio or through the server. In the group’s list of members, a mast marks those with a LoRa radio or who reach you by radio.
  • Screen on. In “More” → “Settings” → “Screen always on”: with the switch green, the screen does not turn off while Tesel is open. It uses more battery.
  • Full screen. In Chrome and similar browsers, on Android and on a computer, Tesel fills the whole screen, without the browser’s bars, from the first tap. The back gesture or the Esc key leave it until Tesel next opens; to never use it, “More” → “Settings” → “Full screen”.
  • Alarm. “More” → “Settings” → “Alarm sound” turns the beep of SOS calls off or on; the vibration stays. With “More” → “Settings” → “Notifications”, the alarm also arrives with Tesel in the background, where the browser allows it; tapping it opens Tesel.
  • Language. Tesel speaks the browser’s language, Spanish or English. It is changed in “More” → “Settings” → “Language”, and remembered.

Afterwards: the diary

“More” → “Operation diary” tells everything that has happened in the group, in order: who joined, the messages and SOS calls, who received them, and every marker or zone added, changed or deleted. “Download” gives it to you as a text file for the report, with coordinates in your format.

In the same panel, under “Encrypted log”, you can save a copy of the group’s log: all its events, encrypted, readable only by members. Loaded on another device of the group, it brings that device whatever it is missing. Use it as a backup, or to hand the coordinator, on a memory stick, what a phone lived through by radio only.

When there is no internet

First of all: you do not need to change anything in the app. Tesel uses whatever link there is. What you write offline is kept and goes out as soon as there is one.

At the top right, Tesel always says what it is connected through: “WiFi”, “4G/5G” or “LoRa”, or several at once (“WiFi · LoRa”), with a green dot; “Connecting…” with an orange dot, or “No connection” with a red one. Where the phone does not say whether it uses Wi-Fi or mobile data (on iPhone, for instance), it shows “WiFi/5G”. Tap it for a card per connection.

If you have a radio

Tap the connection indicator at the top, and in “Connections” tap “Connect radio”. From then on your messages, position and SOS go out by radio and the group's reach you, and the indicator says “LoRa”. The radio does not need to be in sight: it can stay in your backpack. What you wrote without the radio goes out as soon as you connect it, and if the connection drops, Tesel brings it back by itself.

If someone has a radio and coverage

Their phone acts as a bridge without them doing anything: what arrives by radio goes up to the server, and what the server has goes out by radio. Whoever has a radio but no coverage keeps up with what the others write, and the other way round.

If you have neither

The downloaded map keeps working and you can write: messages go out by themselves when the connection returns. If someone in your group has a radio, stay close to them.

Radios

Tesel uses MeshCore radios: small devices that connect to your phone by Bluetooth and send messages to each other over LoRa, a radio signal that travels very far on very little power. In Europe they use the licence-free 868 MHz band.

  • Which models: we are validating radios in the field and will publish recommended models here once testing is done.
  • Setting it up: done once, from a computer with Chrome, with the official MeshCore web flasher and the “Companion Bluetooth” firmware.
  • Range: from about a kilometre in a city to tens of kilometres between high places. Radios repeat each other's messages, so the more there are, the further messages travel. A radio on a high balcony or by a window helps the whole team.
  • Why it is slower: the radio sends very little data, and the law limits how long it may transmit. Tesel puts urgent things first: SOS calls, then messages and finally positions.
  • MeshCore messages: if someone nearby uses the MeshCore app, their messages appear in the chat's “MeshCore” tab so they are not lost. To answer, use the MeshCore app.

If you have an iPhone

Over the internet Tesel works the same on iPhone. What Safari does not allow is connecting a radio by Bluetooth. Until the native app arrives, use the Bluefy browser, which does allow it. It is a stopgap we are still checking.

Privacy and security

  • Everything in the group is encrypted with a key only its members have. The server cannot read the messages.
  • The invitation is the key to the group. Whoever has it gets in. If it was shared with the wrong person, the coordinator can create a new group and invite everyone again. Inside the group everyone is trusted: the app does not check who wrote each message, so a member could pass for another.
  • The group's keys are stored on your phone. Protect it with a screen lock. Leaving a group deletes it from the phone.
  • Over radio the encryption is simpler: it keeps out casual listeners, not someone with means and determination. Do not send by radio what you would not say aloud in a public place.
  • Your position is shared only when you choose.

If something does not work

The top bar says “Offline”

Your phone has no internet. What you write is kept and goes out later. If you have a radio, connect it.

The map is blank

Without internet only areas downloaded beforehand show. Download your area in “Offline maps” next time you have Wi-Fi.

The radio does not show up when connecting

Check that it is on, charged and running the “Companion Bluetooth” firmware, and that you use Chrome or Edge. If it was connected to the MeshCore app, disconnect it there first.

The radio says it has no free channel

Open the MeshCore app, delete a channel you do not use and connect again from Tesel. Tesel never deletes channels by itself.
