Teselia guide

Everything you need to use Teselia with your group: how to get ready, what to do when something happens and how to stay in touch without coverage. No jargon.

1. What Teselia is

Teselia is a map your group shares. On it everyone sees where the others are, what has been marked (a meeting point, a hazard, an area) and the group's messages. If someone needs help, they press SOS and the whole group knows at once, with their position.

With internet it works like any messaging app. Without internet it keeps working in two ways: with pocket radios that pass messages to each other, or through a command post, a computer with its own Wi-Fi at the scene.

Teselia does not replace 112.

It helps your group coordinate. If you can call emergency services, always do.

2. Before: get your group ready

An emergency is no time to learn an app. Set aside half an hour on a quiet day.

Everyone

  1. Open teselia.eu/app in your phone's browser and type your name. No email, phone number or password needed.
  2. Add it to your home screen. On Android, with Chrome: menu ⋮ → “Add to Home screen”. On iPhone, with Safari: Share button → “Add to Home Screen”. It then opens like an app and works offline.
  3. Download the map of your area: “Offline maps” button, move the map to your area and tap “Download visible area”. Do it on Wi-Fi, as it takes space.

The coordinator

  1. Create the group in “Groups” → “Create a group”. Whoever creates it is its coordinator: they can invite others and send alerts.
  2. Tap “Invite” and share the link, or let people scan the QR code. Whoever has the invitation joins the group, so share it only with those who belong.
  3. If you can, get a few radios and set them up (see Radios).

The whole group

  • Agree on a simple plan: a meeting point, who coordinates and what to do without coverage.
  • Run a test together: send a message, send a test SOS and cancel it, and if you have radios, repeat it with phones in airplane mode and Bluetooth on.
  • Repeat the test now and then, with charged batteries.

3. During: what to do

  • Share your position with “My position” → “Share”. You can pause it (the group keeps your last position) or hide it at any time.
  • Write to the group with the “Chat” button. Keep messages short and clear: over radio they cost less and arrive sooner.
  • Mark what matters on the map: a meeting point, a hazard, a closed area. The whole group sees it at once.
  • If you need help, press SOS, choose what is happening and send it. It carries your position. When someone sees it they tap “Acknowledge” and you will know. If it is no longer needed, cancel it.
  • If you see someone else's SOS, tap “Acknowledge” so they know the group has seen it.
  • Coordinators can send alerts from the chat. Critical alerts stay on screen until each person confirms them.

4. When there is no internet

First of all: you do not need to change anything in the app. Teselia uses whatever path there is. What you write offline is kept and goes out as soon as there is a path.

If you have a radio

Tap “LoRa” at the top and “Connect radio”. From then on your messages, position and SOS go out by radio and the group's reach you. The radio does not need to be in sight: it can stay in your backpack.

If there is a command post nearby

Join its Wi-Fi (whoever set it up has the name and password) and open Teselia as usual. At the top you will see an orange dot and “Command post”: you are in touch with the group. The next section explains it.

If you have neither

The downloaded map keeps working and you can write: messages go out by themselves when the connection returns or when you get near a command post. If someone in your group has a radio, stay close to them.

5. The command post

A command post is a laptop or a Raspberry Pi (a small computer) that someone in the group brings to the scene. It does three things:

  1. It creates its own Wi-Fi. It works without internet or phone masts, like a home Wi-Fi with no connection to the outside.
  2. It is a meeting point for messages. Phones on its Wi-Fi pass messages through it, even without a radio. This is how people with only an iPhone, or no radio, stay included.
  3. It is a bridge. With a radio connected at the post, what arrives by radio reaches the Wi-Fi and the other way round. And when the post gets internet back (by cable, satellite or a phone with data), it sends everything to the Teselia server and collects what the rest of the group wrote.

Think of it as the neighbourhood post box.

Everyone drops off their letters and picks up theirs. When the postman (internet) comes by, the box hands everything over and takes in what comes from outside. The letters are sealed: the post cannot read the messages, it only keeps and delivers them.

How to join a command post

  • If you already have Teselia and are in the group: join the post's Wi-Fi and open the app. That is all. You will see “Command post” at the top.
  • If you do not have Teselia: join the post's Wi-Fi, open the address you are given in the browser (for example puesto.teselia.eu/app), choose your name and in “Groups” tap “Scan QR code” to read the coordinator's invitation.
  • If your phone says the Wi-Fi “has no internet”, that is expected: choose to stay connected. If you still have mobile data and the app does not reach the post, turn mobile data off while you are there.

Who sets up the post

Someone comfortable with computers prepares it in advance, following these instructions. Once prepared, at the scene it only needs switching on. It needs internet at least once every two months to renew its security certificate.

The command post is new and we are still testing it in real conditions.

6. Radios

Teselia uses MeshCore radios: small devices that connect to your phone by Bluetooth and send messages to each other over LoRa, a radio signal that travels very far on very little power. In Europe they use the licence-free 868 MHz band.

  • Which to buy: to start, the SenseCAP T1000-E, card-sized with a battery that lasts days. More models are in the list of supported radios.
  • Setting it up: done once, from a computer with Chrome, with the official MeshCore flasher. The steps are in the radio guide.
  • Range: from about a kilometre in a city to tens of kilometres between high places. Radios repeat each other's messages, so the more there are, the further messages travel. A radio on a high balcony or by a window helps the whole group.
  • Why it is slower: the radio sends very little data, and the law limits how long it may transmit. Teselia puts urgent things first: SOS calls, then alerts, then messages and finally positions.
  • MeshCore messages: if someone nearby uses the MeshCore app, their messages appear in the chat's “MeshCore” tab so they are not lost. To answer, use the MeshCore app.

7. If you have an iPhone

Over the internet Teselia works the same on iPhone. What Safari does not allow is connecting a radio by Bluetooth. Until the App Store app arrives, in an emergency you have two options:

  • Join a command post.
  • Try the Bluefy browser, which does allow Bluetooth. It is a stopgap we are still checking.

8. Privacy and security

  • Everything in the group is encrypted with a key only its members have. Neither the server nor a command post can read the messages.
  • The invitation is the key to the group. Whoever has it gets in. If it was shared with the wrong person, the coordinator can create a new group and invite everyone again.
  • The group's keys are stored on your phone. Protect it with a screen lock. Leaving a group deletes it from the phone.
  • Over radio the encryption is simpler: it keeps out casual listeners, not someone with means and determination. Do not send by radio what you would not say aloud in a public place.
  • Your position is shared only when you choose.

9. If something does not work

The top bar says “Offline”

Your phone has no internet. What you write is kept and goes out later. If you have a radio, connect it; if there is a command post, join its Wi-Fi.

The map is blank

Without internet only areas downloaded beforehand show. Download your area in “Offline maps” next time you have Wi-Fi.

The radio does not show up when connecting

Check that it is on, charged and running the “Companion Bluetooth” firmware, and that you use Chrome or Edge. If it was connected to the MeshCore app, disconnect it there first.

The radio says it has no free channel

Open the MeshCore app, delete a channel you do not use and connect again from Teselia. Teselia never deletes channels by itself.

I am on the post's Wi-Fi but it does not connect

Turn mobile data off and open the app again. The first time, the browser may ask for permission to access devices on the local network: allow it.
